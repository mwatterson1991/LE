# Carousel virality + "What does it take to get an MBA?" — research notes

Researched 25 Nov 2026. Evidence strength: STRONG = platform statement or large published dataset. MEDIUM = one credible dataset, or old data. WEAK = small sample / vendor's own numbers. FOLKLORE = repeated advice with no data found.

| Finding | Numbers | Source | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|

### By the parts asked about

- **First-slide hook.** No study isolates it. What is solid: sends and likes per reach drive ranking, and slide 1 is what most of the reach sees. A question as the hook is consistent with the brief; "question hooks outperform" is FOLKLORE as a stated rule (the 37% figure is for questions in captions and comments only).
- **Slide 2.** STRONG: Instagram re-serves unswiped carousels starting on slide 2. Slide 2 has to stand alone.
- **Slide count.** Only 2020 data. Six slides sits in the dip that study found (slides 4–7). Honest read: no current evidence that 6 is worse or better than 8–10.
- **Text density.** No data found either way. FOLKLORE.
- **Save / share triggers.** STRONG that carousels are the save format and that saved carousels are reference material: lists, frameworks, step-by-step. STRONG that sends are the top signal for reaching non-followers. A data table people screenshot or send to a friend who is applying fits both.
- **Video as slide one.** MEDIUM-WEAK: mixed carousels did best in 2020. Mosseri's audio advice is STRONG. A carousel with music can enter the Reels tab (MEDIUM, paraphrased).
- **Last-slide CTA.** No data found. FOLKLORE on Instagram. On LinkedIn, explicit "comment X" asks are something LinkedIn says it demotes.
- **Caption.** Question in caption: +37% comments (Metricool, STRONG for comments). Hashtags: correlated with fewer views (MEDIUM). LinkedIn: shorter captions on documents slightly better (AuthoredUp).
- **Timing.** Midweek, morning or noon local. Small effect.
- **Re-serving.** Covered above (slide 2), plus 70% of views land in the first three days.

Not fetched: Metricool's own press release (blocked), van der Blom's report itself, Later and Hootsuite carousel studies (nothing current and data-backed surfaced in search).
